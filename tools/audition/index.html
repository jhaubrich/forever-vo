      What these four do

      They shape how a line is delivered. They do not decide the accent or the character of the voice — that comes from the clips the reference is cut from, below.

      Emotion
      exaggeration      		0–2. A real conditioning input to the model (emotion_adv): how much expression to put in. It is one number scaled through a single layer, so past the range the model learned it extrapolates rather than emotes, and faster than it seems it should:
      0.3low affect
      0.5default
      0.7colourful
      1.2angry
      1.5shouty
      Higher also speeds a line up; a lower Adherence slows it back down.
      Adherence (pace)
      cfg_weight      		0–1. Classifier-free guidance. At each token the model predicts twice, once with your text and reference and once without, and pushes away from the second: logits = cond + cfg × (cond − uncond). Higher tracks the prompt harder, and clips pauses as it goes; lower is Chatterbox's own advice for a rushed voice, which is why the tuned voices here sit at 0.3. At 0 there is no guidance at all.
      Tempo0.5–2.0. Not a model knob — Chatterbox has no pace control. A pitch-preserving stretch applied when the mp3 is written, and the stretched length is what the addon pages text against. Try a lower Adherence first.
      Pitch−12–12 semitones. Not a model knob either: a shift that keeps the length, applied when the mp3 is written. Chatterbox pulls every clone toward its own mid-range voice, so a deep speaker comes out high — Varimathras's takes measured 153 Hz against his recordings' 86–89, and −3 to −5 sounded right where matching the number (−9) did not. The model runs once per take; every tempo and pitch here is cut from that same audio, so a sweep compares one delivery.

      The two interact: guidance amplifies whatever the conditioning says, so raising Emotion and Adherence compounds. The pairing in this pack — 0.75 Emotion with 0.3 Adherence — is deliberately the other way round: more expression, less pressure on it. In forever-vo.toml they keep Chatterbox's names, exaggeration and cfg_weight.

      Not an accent control. Four settings pairs over eight takes of goblin-male moved its accent not at all, while changing the source clips took the same voice from “sounds like a gnome” to level with the shipped pack.

        What the model actually reads

        Chatterbox reads the reference twice, with different budgets, and the rest barely counts:

        first 6st3, the text→token model — rhythm, stress, pace, accent. How it is said.
        first 10ss3gen, the decoder — timbre, grain, weight. How it sounds.
        whole clipone averaged speaker vector. Weak — a 28s clip is not 28s of influence.
        • Best clip first. The first six seconds do most of the work, and the first clip owns most of them.
        • Fill six seconds. Under that, t3 gets a partial window and falls back on its own prior, which is how an accent goes missing.
        • Connected speech at the head. A sentence carries rhythm; “Hello there!” carries none. Shouts and death cries at the head are what this panel exists to fix.
        • 6–10s only shapes timbre. The speaker's own greetings are fine there.
        • Past 10s is nearly free and nearly useless. Do not mistake a long clip for a strong one — watch the pills, not the total.
        • One speaker across the first 10s. Mixing two actors made goblin-male worse the more of the second went in.
        • The source clips matter more than the settings. Exaggeration and cfg_weight are worth having, but they did not move the accent: four settings over eight takes of goblin-male changed nothing, while changing which clips fed it took the voice from “sounds like a gnome” to level with the shipped pack. Fix the clip first; reach for the knobs after.
        • A strong regional accent may not survive at all. Fifteen different head clips, four settings and bark-free references all failed to carry retail goblin's New York. Timbre and delivery transfer reliably; an accent the model has no prior for may not, whatever you feed it.
